Watch Party

A shared YouTube queue with one person driving. The party lives in its own window so it can go on a second monitor; the main client keeps a strip above the voice panel to join, leave and see what is playing. Nothing here is wired up — every control is a picture.

Who can start one

Anyone. The + next to the section header is live for every member, not just admins — unlike text and voice channels, which are the server's furniture.

Why a strip, not a channel

A watch party is something you are in, like a call. Putting it where the voice panel is means one glance answers "what am I currently in".

Voice keeps running

The party never touches the call. People stay in 🔊 General and talk over the video, so the party needs no audio transport of its own.

Surface 2 — its own window

The party window

A separate OS window, freely moved and resized onto whatever monitor you like, holding the player, the queue and a chat that exists only while the party does. Flip the switch to see what a guest may touch and what only the host may.

Message, or paste a YouTube link to queue it ↵
Chat here is gone when the party ends — it never reaches #general.

One composer, two jobs

Paste a link and it becomes a queue item; type anything else and it is a message. No separate "add video" field to hunt for, and the queued line in chat is the receipt.

Removing from the queue

You always get an ✕ on your own items. The host gets one on everything, drawn in amber so it is clear that is a host power and not your own.

Volume is per person

The bar in the transport is the video's, for you only. Clicking a watcher opens the per-user volume the voice sidebar already has, so one slider covers both.

What we still have to decide

Each of these changes the build meaningfully, and none of them are settled by a mockup.

Sync accuracyhardest part
YouTube's iframe player takes only coarse commands, so frame-perfect lock is out. The realistic target is everyone within about a second: the server holds the clock, clients nudge with playback rate on small drift and hard-seek on large. Buffering is the ugly case — does one person's stall pause the room, or do they catch up alone?
Host handoverwho drives
If the host closes the window or drops off, someone inherits the controls. Simplest rule that works: the longest-present watcher becomes host, with a line in party chat saying so. The party ends when the last person leaves.
"Anyone can have their audio adjusted"needs your call
Two readings, and the mockup shows both. Either everyone sets their own video volume locally (the transport bar), or watchers can be turned up and down individually the way voice members already can (the popover). If it is the second, is that simply the existing per-user voice volume under a new lid?
Where the state livesserver shape
Nothing here needs to survive a restart, so: in-memory on the server, one party object per guild, broadcast over the socket gateway that already exists. No Prisma migration, no new tables, and a server restart simply ends the party.
One party or manyscope
Starting with one live party per server keeps the sidebar honest and dodges "which party am I in". Several at once is a later change, not a shape we have to leave room for now.
Blocked embedswill happen
Plenty of videos refuse to play embedded, and it fails per person — you find out when your own player errors. The queue needs a state for "this one will not play here", surfaced to the host so they can skip it rather than everyone staring at a black rectangle.
